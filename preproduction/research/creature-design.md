# Wizards on a Boat — creature research and proposed bestiary

Preproduction proposal, 16 September 2026. These are original concepts for review, not approved production assets. All dimensions, probabilities, names and encounter rules below are starting hypotheses. Habitat and dungeon names match the current [art manifest](../manifest.js) and biome/dungeon prompts: four sea, four land and three sky biomes. All names remain provisional until review.

## What the references actually support

**Subnautica: emotion before feature accumulation.** Charlie Cleveland's GDC 2019 session is explicitly about exploration, discovery and uncertainty, using design pillars, prototypes, phobias and light guidance to structure a sandbox. The official session description and video listing were verified; this research does not claim a complete viewing or an unverified timestamp analysis. [GDC session](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1025745/The-Design-of-Subnautica), [official GDC recording](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7R-x9NSBS2Y).

**Subnautica: a related, plausible fauna.** In his firsthand account, creature artist Alex Ries describes connecting Below Zero's creatures to the original fauna through shared evolution, while keeping both comic and frightening designs plausible within the game's tone. The useful lesson is consistency between species and environments, rather than copying any particular animal. [Alex Ries, October 2024](https://www.vol1brooklyn.com/2024/10/23/alex-ries-on-the-creation-of-other-worlds/).

**Subnautica: memorable material sources.** An early Unknown Worlds design post explains why abstract, widely scattered ingredients made gathering unclear. It describes making sources more specific, strengthening creature materials and giving dangerous deeper areas distinctive rewards. This is evidence of a historical design decision, not a claim that every current Subnautica recipe follows it. [Unknown Worlds loot and tech-tree design, February 2015](https://unknownworlds.com/en/news/new-loot-system-tech-tree-design).

**DREDGE: design the place and encounter together.** Black Salt Games artist Michael Bastiaens describes establishing a biome and its gameplay obstruction before developing its monster. Designs changed through testing: shallow water, turning space, night visibility and scope all affected what worked. Rough blockouts established scale before detailed models; rigs and animations continued changing in context. This firsthand explanation supports starting with a creature's job in a particular place and validating its shape there. [Michael Bastiaens, November 2023](https://80.lv/articles/tidal-terrors-designing-the-monsters-of-dredge).

**Useful further viewing.** Noclip's direct developer documentary includes published chapters for art style at 6:30, biomes at 10:04, gameplay tension at 16:46 and fish design at 25:46. These chapter labels and times were verified from Noclip's listing; no additional claims here depend on unseen video content. [The Design of Dredge, August 2024](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yXQipk_mBAo).

## Our adaptation to the supplied style board

These are our proposed decisions, not statements made by the reference developers.

1. **Wonder is the default.** Most distant animal encounters should reward looking. Danger arrives through behavior, weather and scale, without making the entire ocean visually hostile.
2. **A creature has a life before it has an attack.** Define feeding, resting, habitat and avoidance before combat. A grazing animal can become defensive; a predator can lose interest.
3. **One strong anatomical idea per species.** A folding throat lamp, inflatable flotation lobes or a shell that stores pressure is more memorable than many unrelated spikes, eyes and appendages.
4. **Let biology explain the item.** A venting crab gives a pressure valve; a salt grazer gives crystallized salt. The material's name and icon should suggest where to find it.
5. **Related species share shape grammar.** The crab and orchard boss share rounded plated shells and articulated small feeding claws. Sky fauna repeat webbing and inflatable forms where they serve flight.
6. **Threats alter decisions.** One encounter asks the crew to extinguish bright lights, another to sail across a current, another to land and shelter. Avoid making every creature a faster pursuer with more health.
7. **A readable warning precedes consequences.** Combine silhouette or pose, a sound, and an environmental cue. Color alone must never carry the warning.
8. **Keep the reference's material language.** Broad faceted planes, painted color grouping, warm ivory highlights, navy shadows, turquoise water and sparse ochre accents. Avoid wet photographic skin, hair-strand detail and gore.
9. **Reserve visual complexity for landmarks.** Small animals use two or three masses and restrained patterning; bosses can carry asymmetry and habitat growth while retaining a clear outer contour.
10. **Respect the laptop.** These ideas should work as a small number of active animals, cheap distant silhouettes and authored animation states. A convincing ecology does not require continuous simulation of the whole world.

## Sheet specification

Each creature needs one approval sheet showing an unobstructed front, side and rear view of the same anatomy, plus a three-quarter behavior pose, a small material/appendage detail and a wizard silhouette for scale. Use consistent neutral daylight so the same creature can later work in more than one biome. At least one silhouette thumbnail should be readable without interior texture. For aerial and aquatic species, a top view may replace the rear view if it explains anatomy better, but include the missing orientation in a later modeling handoff.

The sheet is concept guidance, not guaranteed orthographic modeling truth. Generated images must be checked for mismatched limb counts, changing markings, contradictory joints and impossible back views before any model is commissioned. Spell effects must stay in the behavior inset, not obscure the base anatomy.

## Proposed bestiary

### C01 — Ribbonwake Glider

- **Role / ecology:** Peaceful filter feeder following plankton-rich surface currents. Small groups trace safe approaches to reef channels, giving explorers a living navigation clue.
- **Shape:** Approximately 2 m across. A compact kite-shaped body, four broad petal fins, sail-like filtering throat and one long curled ribbon tail. Broad ivory back, turquoise margins, navy underside, tiny amber eyes.
- **Habitat / time / rarity:** Sunlit Shoals and warm reef edges; dawn and daytime; common, with an uncommon pale morph. The morph changes body markings and behavior confidence, not the species' core anatomy.
- **Behavior / telegraph:** Fins fold vertically and the group tightens before fleeing nearby predators. A soft paired whistle accompanies the movement.
- **Spell interaction:** A mild wind impulse can briefly redirect a glider; strong repeated impacts scatter the school. It is an observation opportunity, not a required combat target.
- **Inventory proposal:** 1–2 glider scales at 65%; one ribbon membrane at 20%. The membrane is species-exclusive. A nonlethal shed-scale encounter is possible as a separate reward event. No spell unlocks.
- **Sheet emphasis:** Clearly distinguish its two pairs of fins; show the filtering throat closed and gently deployed.

### C02 — Kettleback Crab

- **Role / ecology:** A tide-pool scavenger that draws warm mineral water into a shell chamber, then expels it to clear sand and expose food.
- **Shape:** Approximately 1.2 m wide. Six walking legs and two small feeding claws beneath a low cream dome; two swept organic chimney vents; cobalt underside and ochre joints. Vents grow from shell and must not look like metal plumbing.
- **Habitat / time / rarity:** Basalt Heath's geothermal shores and tide pools, including island edges facing Storm Reach; active around low tide at any hour; common locally, rare away from vent habitat.
- **Behavior / telegraph:** Rocks backward, locks feet, then produces three shell clicks and two expanding steam puffs before a short pressure burst.
- **Spell interaction:** A correctly timed wind or cold spell can redirect or interrupt pressure release. Ordinary combat is optional around its feeding territory.
- **Inventory proposal:** 1–3 shell chips at 85%; one kettle valve at 25%; one salt pearl at 5%. Kettle valve is exclusive, used for mundane equipment or potion crafting. No spell unlocks.
- **Sheet emphasis:** Lock the six-leg/two-claw count and show the rear chamber and vent paths.

### C03 — Lanternjaw Skulker

- **Role / ecology:** A crepuscular ambush predator that uses a throat light to attract small animals around kelp edges and wrecks. Bright moving deck lights can resemble a feeding event.
- **Shape:** Approximately 4 m long. Low eel/seal torso, blunt shovel snout, one paired set of crescent forefins and a paddle tail. An amber lamp folds beneath the jaw like a flexible dewlap. No exposed humanlike teeth or gore.
- **Habitat / time / rarity:** Kelp Channels' deep margins and drowned wreck fields at the edge of Lantern Trench; dusk and night; uncommon, with a rare old scarred individual.
- **Behavior / telegraph:** Light changes from slow pulses to a held glow, forefins spread and a low ripple advances ahead of the body before a committed lunge.
- **Spell interaction:** Light decoys can draw it away; a sideways impulse can spoil a lunge. It returns to cover after a missed attempt rather than pursuing indefinitely.
- **Inventory proposal:** 1–2 tough hides at 80%; one lantern gland at 30%; one luminous lens at 6%. Lens is exclusive. Materials may be trade goods or potion ingredients, not glyph unlocks.
- **Sheet emphasis:** Show the exact folded/deployed throat mechanism and side silhouette at water level.

### C04 — Tidebell Colossus — sea boss

- **Role / ecology:** A huge mollusk that filters nutrient pulses where deep currents meet ancient submerged masonry. Its slow pumping creates powerful periodic currents; the fight interrupts a territorial feeding cycle.
- **Shape:** Approximately 14 m tall including limbs. One ivory bell-shaped shell with broad stepped teal ridges, six thick dark-teal arms and an ochre mantle fringe; one visible eye on each side. Organic ridges evoke a bell without literal towers, windows or artificial ornament.
- **Habitat / time / rarity:** A deep-current confluence in Lantern Trench beside the main encounter region of The Tide Vault; one boss encounter per relevant dungeon region; surfaces during a local tide event. Unique boss status is separate from ordinary rarity rolls. The starter annex remains separate and accessible without this encounter.
- **Behavior / telegraph:** The mantle seals, shell ridges hum and a conspicuous ring draws inward before a suction phase. Outflow follows a clearly different shell-open pose.
- **Crew problem:** Steer across the flow, place wind impulses into a reefed sail and interrupt exposed mantle valves. Reward timing and coordination; do not require a single fixed spell recipe.
- **Inventory proposal:** 2–4 bell-shell plates guaranteed; one resonant pearl at 35%; one unique Pressure effect page guaranteed on first eligible victory. Repeat victories use a material reward policy to be decided. Pressure unlocks never enter the shop pool.
- **Sheet emphasis:** Under-shell anatomy and arm attachment matter as much as the iconic shell. Add tiny boat and wizard scale references.

### C05 — Brinecap Browser

- **Role / ecology:** A gentle shoreline herbivore. It eats salt-heavy plants and sheds excess minerals through a broad dorsal fan, leaving harvestable deposits near resting sites.
- **Shape:** Approximately 1.4 m long. Stout four-legged body, webbed feet, short scoop muzzle, a single broad fan of blunt salt-crystal plates across its back. Moss green and ivory with muted ochre nose.
- **Habitat / time / rarity:** Saltgrass Coast and Mangrove Orchard margins; dawn and afternoon; common. A rare mature form has a taller fan and rests farther inland near vegetated Glass Dunes edges.
- **Behavior / telegraph:** Scrapes the ground, lowers the fan and stamps before a defensive shove. Normally retreats toward cover.
- **Spell interaction:** Gentle water and wind can move a herd from a blocked path; avoid framing all physics contact as damage.
- **Inventory proposal:** 1–3 brine crystals at 80%; one capstone salt at 15%. Capstone salt is exclusive. Rest-site deposits offer a second acquisition route with their own cooldown. No spell unlocks.
- **Sheet emphasis:** Crystal fan is blunt and integrated with the animal, with a believable neck turn and resting pose.

### C06 — Basalt Knuckler

- **Role / ecology:** A territorial mineral-forager that turns loose rocks to reach grubs and heat-loving roots. Its impacts make natural paths temporarily hazardous.
- **Shape:** Approximately 2.3 m long. A low six-limbed lizard: two massive front knuckles, four smaller walking feet, layered dark basalt-like plates and an ochre expandable throat. Tail is short and acts as a brace.
- **Habitat / time / rarity:** Basalt Heath's volcanic uplands and hot ravines; daytime basking, most active near sunset; uncommon.
- **Behavior / telegraph:** Tests the ground with two taps, raises its front body, then strikes. Pebbles jump before a larger slam.
- **Spell interaction:** Wind can spoil its balance during the raised pose; water reduces access to a hot feeding patch and encourages relocation. Rock movement is limited to tagged loose objects.
- **Inventory proposal:** 1–3 basalt plates at 85%; one knuckle geode at 18%; one warm heartstone at 4%. Knuckle geode is exclusive, a crafting material rather than a spell page.
- **Sheet emphasis:** Make the oversized forelimbs legible from the front; avoid accidental gorilla anatomy.

### C07 — Orchard Reliquary — land boss

- **Role / ecology:** An ancient beetle-tortoise carrying a symbiotic grove. Small plants grow in its porous shell and attract pollinators; it protects a nest in the irrigated courtyard of an overgrown library ruin.
- **Shape:** Approximately 6 m high. A domed ochre shell, four pillar legs, two small feeding claws, a broad seed-pod crown and teal barklike seams. The grove is a few deliberate large branches, not an unreadable mass of foliage.
- **Habitat / time / rarity:** Mangrove Orchard surrounding The Root Archive; one boss per major grove; wakes when the archive's sealed chamber is disturbed, regardless of time.
- **Behavior / telegraph:** Leaves turn and fold, pods rattle and the animal roots its forefeet before a seed volley or ground shove. A different lifted-shell pose precedes movement.
- **Crew problem:** Redirect seed pods, open irrigation sluices and expose cooled shell seams. Preserve the broad readable creature beneath environmental dressing.
- **Inventory proposal:** 2–4 living bark plates guaranteed; one elder seed at 40%; one unique Bind modifier page guaranteed on first eligible victory. The page belongs to the boss/dungeon progression pool and is never sold by the shop.
- **Sheet emphasis:** Show underside, leg joints and shell without foliage in a small inset. Preserve one clear animal body, not a walking building.

### C08 — Threadwing Skater

- **Role / ecology:** An aerial seed-eating insect that catches crosswinds between floating islands. Its group flight makes invisible air lanes visible.
- **Shape:** Approximately 1 m wingspan. Four fabric-like delta wings around a narrow navy body, a long balancing fork tail and two dangling grasping feet; ivory and turquoise wing panels with ochre seams.
- **Habitat / time / rarity:** Cloud Meadow and its open sky-island edges; daytime fair weather; common in small flocks.
- **Behavior / telegraph:** Wings snap into a diamond and the tail points downwind before a rapid lateral skip. It darts away from disturbance.
- **Spell interaction:** A wind burst can reveal an airflow direction through flock reaction. A cloud of skaters should remain a visual/navigation event, not a costly flock simulation.
- **Inventory proposal:** 1–2 wing threads at 60%; one balancing quill at 12%. Balancing quill is exclusive. Naturally snagged threads can also occur around roosts. No spell unlocks.
- **Sheet emphasis:** Four wings and two feet must be consistent in every angle; wings are membranous, not literal sewn fabric.

### C09 — Cumulus Grazer

- **Role / ecology:** A slow floating herbivore that anchors to cliff vegetation, browsing cloud-moss and dispersing seeds. Biological gas chambers provide fantasy buoyancy with a consistent internal rule.
- **Shape:** Approximately 3 m long. Rounded teal body surrounded by four inflatable ivory lobes, a navy face, two short steering paddles and a long anchoring tail. Broad clean forms, not a sheep pasted onto a cloud.
- **Habitat / time / rarity:** Astral Orchard's sheltered ledges and cloud-moss undersides, with occasional groups on suitable Cloud Meadow shelves; dawn and overcast days; uncommon.
- **Behavior / telegraph:** Deflates two lobes, curls its tail and produces a descending whistle before a defensive downward gust.
- **Spell interaction:** Wind gently pushes it between perches; repeated hard pushes cause retreat. Show that it can anchor rather than letting physics send it into endless drift.
- **Inventory proposal:** 1–2 buoyant fibers at 75%; one cloud bladder at 20%; one condensed dew bead at 7%. Cloud bladder is exclusive and used in equipment/potions. No spell unlocks.
- **Sheet emphasis:** Show inflation and tail attachment; keep the four-lobe plan readable from above.

### C10 — Stormglass Roc

- **Role / ecology:** A large sky predator that hunts along storm edges. Conductive mineral deposits in its feathers build static charge as it flies through charged air.
- **Shape:** Approximately 5 m wingspan. A broad seabird plan with swept angular turquoise primaries, forked tail and amber glasslike throat crest. Dark blue upper body, ivory chest; large sparse feather groups.
- **Habitat / time / rarity:** Stormglass Reef sky islands; daylight storms and storm-clearance periods; rare. This is an elite creature, not one of the two progression bosses.
- **Behavior / telegraph:** Holds wings rigid, crest crackles and circles once with a loud descending call before diving. An obvious static wake follows the dive path.
- **Spell interaction:** Crew shelter, grounding structures and sideways wind offer counters. It should disengage after defending its feeding patch.
- **Inventory proposal:** 1–3 storm feathers at 85%; one glass pinion at 22%; one charged crop stone at 5%. Glass pinion is exclusive. All are material items; no direct spell component or modifier unlocks.
- **Sheet emphasis:** Folded-wing rear view, primary feather grouping and throat crest; avoid visual overlap with Threadwing Skater.

## Spawn, inventory and progression rules proposed for later implementation

- Treat **species availability**, **individual rarity** and **boss status** as separate fields. Biome, elevation/depth, time, weather and local habitat capacity first determine eligible species; a subsequent rarity roll selects a permitted variant. A rare mob must still obey its habitat rules.
- The percentages above are independent per-item example rolls. Each spawned animal owns an actual persistent loot inventory rolled once by the authoritative host; inspecting or reopening it does not reroll the contents. Quantities and exclusivity are data, not special cases embedded in AI behavior.
- Rarity may add a visible anatomical accent and adjust materials, but should not silently change the attack vocabulary. Any new ability needs a new telegraph.
- Keep ordinary materials distinct from **spell components/modifiers that unlock spell construction**. Under the user's rules, the latter are only dungeon and selected-boss rewards. A shop can sell finished staffs with equipment affixes, potions and travel tools without offering these unlock pages.
- First-victory guarantees proposed for bosses reduce the risk that essential progression depends on indefinite random grinding. Optional prestige materials can remain probabilistic. Exact party ownership and duplicate-page conversion need later design review.
- For multiplayer, present one coherent warning phase to the whole crew and assign loot authoritatively. Do not let every client spawn its own reward roll.
- For procedural placement, validate water depth, turn radius, bank clearance and escape space before accepting a creature habitat. If its habitat cannot fit, select a smaller eligible species or leave the location empty. An impressive boss concept does not justify impossible collision geometry.

## Review questions resolved by the art pack

The first review should establish whether the fauna belongs beside the supplied ocean, whether common creatures remain appealing, whether each silhouette communicates a distinct role, and whether the two bosses feel monumental without drifting into horror gore or detailed realism. Combat balance, final spawn rates and production topology follow approval and prototype tests; these sheets must not be treated as proof those systems already work.
